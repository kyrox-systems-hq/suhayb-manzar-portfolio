Drasteon

An agent-native sales operating system designed to analyse commercial context, recommend decisions and perform approved CRM work inside clear authority boundaries.

Context

Conventional CRMs record work. The product idea was to perform it.

Many B2B companies collect large amounts of commercial data but still depend on RevOps specialists, administrators and consultants to turn that data into a reliable action. Drasteon was conceived as an operating layer where agents can reason over governed business context and propose useful work.

The initial wedge is B2B sales, where the underlying records, workflows and economic buyer are clear. The longer-term idea is broader: software that performs specialist work rather than only storing it.

Challenge

An ambitious product needed a credible boundary between AI and authority.

A chatbot beside an ordinary CRM would not be enough. The product needed to explain why agentic action could be useful without pretending that a language model should have unlimited authority over commercial records.

The core design challenge became the control plane: models interpret and propose, while deterministic software validates permissions, policy, approvals, tool contracts and audit evidence.

Approach

Build the architecture and the commercial narrative together.

Make the distinction understandable

The website uses a direct contrast: CRMs record the work, Drasteon does the work. The rest of the page then explains what “does” means through analysis, coaching, approved execution and plain-language automation.

Show a governed workflow

The product story follows a practical sequence from objective to control plane, approval gate and action receipt. It turns an abstract architectural claim into something a buyer or investor can inspect.

Be precise about maturity

The site presents Drasteon as a working architecture prototype, not a finished customer product. It clearly separates what has been validated from what the pre-seed build still needs to deliver.
